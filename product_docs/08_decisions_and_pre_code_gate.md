#### Reference-versus-TensorRT measurements

The same input tensors were sent to OpenCV DNN ONNX and TensorRT FP32. On 200 `MOT17-02-FRCNN` frames at confidence 0.25/NMS 0.45, all 200 person counts matched. Raw output max/mean absolute errors were 0.02550 and 0.00003395; paired restored boxes had maximum coordinate difference 0.273 px, minimum IoU 0.98965, mean IoU 0.99986, and 1,402 box pairs. At the candidate confidence 0.10 with visibility-qualified diagnostics, 198/200 counts matched, maximum coordinate difference was 0.552 px, minimum IoU 0.98965, and mean IoU 0.99977 over 2,734 box pairs. These are diagnostic tolerances, not a supervisor-approved freeze tolerance.

#### Development precision/recall and root-cause checks

The original D-42 diagnostic used only `MOT17-02-FRCNN`. D-44 restored and verified `MOT17-04-FRCNN` and `MOT17-05-FRCNN`; D-46 then ran the frozen candidate for 100 frames per scene. Aggregate confidence 0.10 precision was 61.48% and recall 52.43% across 5,581 visibility-qualified boxes (2,926 TP, 1,833 FP, 2,655 FN). Scene recalls were 43.17% (02), 52.50% (04), and 88.67% (05). OpenCV/TensorRT count parity was 98%, 98%, and 100%. The earlier 200-frame scene-02 results remain: confidence 0.25 produced precision 76.75% and recall 26.62%; confidence 0.10 produced precision 51.74% and recall 44.72%. Confidence 0.05 increased recall only to 49.41% and reduced precision to 36.37%; changing NMS IoU to 0.65 at confidence 0.10 produced 44.03% recall and 38.30% precision.

The high reference box agreement supports the coordinate restoration and letterbox implementation. The detector filters class 0 (person) before NMS, so class leakage is not the observed cause. Lowering confidence exposes more candidates but creates many false positives; NMS changes do not recover the missing recall. The remaining leading risk is checkpoint suitability and/or its unconfirmed pretrained-weight provenance, not TensorRT numerical drift. No final evaluation scene was used for tuning.
